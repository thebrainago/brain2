6   Macro overview – at a glance
    Rajat Bhattacharya
    Senior Investment Strategist



    Key themes
    Core scenario (soft landing, 45% probability): An economic soft landing remains our core scenario, with the global economy
    overcoming this year’s oil price shock thanks to a surge in AI investment in the US and Asia. Record wealth generation driven
    by the stock market rally is supporting US consumption. Fiscal easing in the US, Germany and Japan is supporting growth.
    Meanwhile, inflation has reaccelerated due to a rebound in fuel prices as the Middle East conflict drags on. Against this backdrop,
    the Fed, ECB and BoJ all hiked rates in the same month (in September) for the first time. Although all three subsequently paused
    in October, we expect the Fed to hike twice more, the ECB once more and the BoJ thrice more by June 2027. Nevertheless, we
    expect the energy and tariff effects on inflation to fade by H2 2027, enabling the Fed and the ECB to cut rates by Q3 2027.
    Meanwhile, China is likely to target credit easing and accelerate fiscal spending in H1 2027 to revive domestic growth.

    Upside risk (no landing, 30% probability): We maintain the probability at 30% as AI-driven investment growth continues.
    There is a reasonable chance that lower oil prices next year, the AI-fuelled wealth boom and fiscal easing in Germany and Japan
    will boost ‘animal spirits’. A potential global defence spending boom, led by the US and Europe, could also lift global growth.

    Downside risk (25% probability): We raised the risk of a recession to 15%, especially if central banks are forced to accelerate
    the pace of rate hikes to douse rising inflation. A stock market downturn negating the wealth effect or a bond sell-off due to fiscal
    concerns are other tail risks. We cut stagflation probability to 10% as we see low risk of oil prices sustaining above USD 120/bbl.

7   Fixed Income – at a glance
    Jonathan Liang, CFA             Cedric Lam                            Ray Heung                            Anthony Naab, CFA
    CIO, Fixed Income & FX          Senior Investment Strategist          Senior Investment Strategist         Investment Strategist



    Our view
    Foundation: Bond yields remain near multi-year highs, offering an attractive entry point. We view fixed income as a Core holding
    (ie, a Neutral allocation) in our multi-asset portfolios. Within the asset class, we maintain an Overweight stance on EM USD
    government bonds, given their attractive risk-reward. We hold an Underweight view on Developed Market (DM) government
    bonds due to the tail risks associated with term premia.

    The Fed’s decision to hold rates at 3.75-4.00% in October amid still-elevated energy prices suggests that every Fed meeting
    from here is ‘live’, with a rate change possible on incoming data. With US inflation still running hot and the labour market tight,
    we expect two more 25bps hikes (one by year-end and one in H1 2027), bringing the Fed’s target range for the federal funds
    rate to 4.25-4.5%. We continue to favour the 3-7-year part of the US government bond yield curve as the best hedge against
    any risk-off move driven by AI-related growth fears. As inflation pressures ease in H2 2027, we see room for the Fed to consider
    a rate cut, which should pull nominal yields lower. Solid corporate fundamentals and supportive technicals should keep spreads
    tight, but there is limited room for them to tighten further. We still favour EM USD over local-currency (LCY) government bonds.

    Opportunistic ideas: We are bullish on US TIPS, AAA-rated CLOs, broad US HYs and Australian dollar (AUD) corporate bonds.

   USD view
   USD consolidation with limited upside: Our three-month US Dollar Index (DXY) forecast stands at 101, revised upwards from
   100.20 last month, reflecting a higher expected Fed rate path. With the October Fed rate pause and a potential December rate
   hike largely priced in, further tightening would offer limited additional USD support. Persistent inflation, elevated US government
   bond yields and resilient business activity continue to underpin the USD's interest-rate advantage. However, in September,
   payroll growth slowed sharply to 29,000 (from 133,000 in August), the unemployment rate rose to 4.2% from 4.1%, annual wage
   growth eased to 3.0% from 3.1% and housing starts fell 5.2% m/m. These signs of cooling demand temper expectations of more
   aggressive Fed tightening. We expect DXY to remain rangebound between 101 and 103, with our three-month forecast at 101.00.
   Peaking Fed rates and narrowing rate differentials favour gradual USD weakness: We maintain our 12-month DXY forecast
   at 98.50. We expect a Fed rate hike in December 2026 and another in H1 2027, taking the federal funds target range to a peak
   of 4.25-4.50%, before easing in H2 2027. Further BoJ normalisation and the ECB's relatively restrictive policy stance should
   progressively narrow the USD's rate advantage. Persistent fiscal and current account deficits increase reliance on foreign capital
   inflows, adding to medium-term pressure on the USD. Resilient US growth, AI-related capital inflows and elevated US
   government bond yields remain important offsets, supporting DXY’s gradual decline towards 98.50.

   Summary
   Our stock-bond model (3-6 months) is at -2 (minimum: -5) in October, signalling a modest Underweight (UW) in global
   equities. Historically, a decline in the model score to -2 has been associated with flat to slightly negative returns. The shift was
   driven by stretched valuations in both global and Asian equities, weaker fundamentals and deteriorating market technicals. On
   fundamentals, market risk aversion has risen sharply, while PMI new orders have declined over the past three months, pointing
   to a slowdown in the pace of industrial expansion. On technicals, the share of equity markets trading above their 200-day moving
   averages, a key gauge of long-term support, has fallen below 60% for the first time since January 2025. The model rebalances
   in the middle of each month and has generated 11bps of alpha from the equity Underweight initiated on 15 October 2026, lifting
   YTD alpha vs. the 60/40 equity-bond benchmark to 4.0%.

   Our 1-3-month equity market regime model indicates contained bear market risks across US and global equities.
   Estimated bear market probabilities are 4.2% for the S&P500 and 7.7% for the MSCI AC World, compared with 0.9% and 6.4%
   respectively in the September outlook. Across both models, volatility measures are the main factors keeping probabilities low,
   supported by momentum signals. Together, these outweigh modest upward contributions from macro and cross-market factors,
   leaving little evidence of a bear market regime over the next 1-3 months.

   Our market diversity indicator flags reversal risk in MSCI Europe Real Estate and MSCI China Industrials, which are down
   over 12% and 19% respectively from their August peaks. US equity and USD positioning remain broadly neutral.
