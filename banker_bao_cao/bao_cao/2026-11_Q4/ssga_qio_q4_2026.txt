Introduction
Recursive but still resilient?
As we move into the final quarter of 2026, we think it is good
practice to consider how our views on the economy and
financial markets have changed over the year. This includes
recognizing what we got right and learning where we could
have done better. It’s a process that could be accurately
described as recursive self-improvement: reviewing results
and using those lessons to make better decisions. This mirrors
advancements in artificial intelligence, where AI continually
learns how to build better versions of itself.

Constructively, recursive self-improvement of AI models could
have tremendous productivity benefits for the global economy
and financial rewards for those companies and investors that
get it right. But it poses questions about safety, oversight,
and the future complexion of labor markets. AI’s fast-growing
influence also highlights how economic and market growth is
being driven by a narrow cohort of companies and investment
themes. Further AI advances may only exacerbate this
concentration.

Narrow market drivers alongside fatter upside and downside
tails do not necessarily herald broad market weakness.
However, the range of potential outcomes is widening and
could test the resilience that has underpinned markets through
a rewarding 2026 to date.
